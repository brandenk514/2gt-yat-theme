09 — Signals worth building on

Buried lede

HPE is giving away hardware

One commenter reports HPE approached them offering free hardware to move off VMware onto Morpheus. An MSP is moving every customer to Morpheus. But Morpheus is the only platform in the set with net negative sentiment: unimpressed after evaluation, monthly cold calls from multiple reps, GreenLake support complaints, and "HPE is the next lock-in."

Free hardware plus bad word of mouth is a story. Nobody has covered it.

Counter-narrative

The people staying have reasons

Fifteen commenters say they are staying, and almost none say it is because they are trapped. Full VCF stack with vSAN, NSX and Automation has no single-vendor equivalent. SAP and Cisco VOIP certifications. Legacy SAN that rules out Nutanix. A VDI shop on Omnissa. One org whose increase was only 15% because they were too small to bother squeezing.

Your video's framing assumes leaving is the rational default. Two dozen comments disagree with specifics, and one flatly disputes your Nutanix pricing claim with their own numbers.

Strongest thread, 53 comments

Everyone is waiting for 2027

The single most repeated argument, by a wide margin. Three-year ELAs signed just before the acquisition expire next year. Several add a second trigger: the hardware refresh cycle. One commenter puts full exit at five years and names 2031.

Underweighted

Digital sovereignty is now a buying criterion

Thirteen comments, and it is no longer abstract. Five separately point out Proxmox is Austrian and therefore European. One org went to Huawei FusionCompute in South Africa. A consultant reports Sangfor, Huawei DCS and H3C driving bank migrations across Asia, LatAm and Africa. One commenter moved to Azure Local under a cloud-first strategy and is now being told to abandon US cloud.

Untold, 12 comments

The individuals nobody serves

Workstation Pro refugees running Cisco Modeling Labs, Windows 2000 for a customer, retro OSes, air-gapped labs. One moved to six physical PCs and a hardware KVM because nothing else does 3D passthrough. One asks directly: "What's in it for us individual users? We're stuck with Broadcom?"

Proxmox's real ceiling

Support, not capability

Six criticisms and they cluster. The SLA and support model disqualifies it at companies with contractual SLOs. Multipath iSCSI needs config-file surgery. Snapshot behaviour on shared storage. Doubts about maintenance burden at scale. Notably, a Proxmox user rebuts each one in the replies, including that LVM-on-iSCSI snapshots landed in version 9.

And in this pull, the argument resolved itself. The person who said snapshots were gone came back, acknowledged version 9 fixed it, and said that having sorted their backups out it's now "more of a goodie than a necessity." Two operators worked it out in your replies without you.

Reality check

Nutanix cuts both ways

One org consolidated eight or nine VMware clusters into two Nutanix clusters running 600 to 700 VMs, exactly the pitch Nutanix makes. But three separate operators, unprompted, describe the same failure on the other side: mismatched end-of-life dates meant they could not realize ROI on their hardware, one concluding "AHV only, never HCI again." Paired with a hardware compatibility list strict enough to disqualify the older servers everyone is stuck extending, that is the closest thing in this dataset to a documented pattern rather than a one-off complaint.

Second-order

The cost crisis stacked

VMware's increase collided with RAM and storage prices up 5x and cloud spend doubling. One org's AWS bill went £300k to £1.8m; another €35k to €80k per month. Multiple commenters say they cannot buy new servers at all and are refurbishing old ones, which is itself driving platform choice: Nutanix's strict HCL loses to Proxmox on second-hand hardware.

New in this pull

The certification glut

A commenter running 3,500 VMs raised something nobody else did: the market is about to be saturated with VMware certified engineers, because everyone got certified when it was the only platform and now there's nowhere to use it.

Same comment, better detail: one of their global offices emailed asking for support and discovered their licenses had expired the previous year. Nobody had budgeted for it. That is a failure mode nobody is talking about, and at that org's scale it cannot be unique.

New in this pull

OpenShift's clean record just broke

Until this pull OpenShift had nine pieces of praise and one criticism, all of it from Red Hat shops saying it was hard but worth it. Now there's an operator saying large VMs with terabyte virtual disks on Pure Storage killed productivity: any latency causes Windows guests to blue screen or orphan inodes. Their verdict was that it isn't built for enterprises.

Every alternative in this dataset now has at least one detailed failure report from somebody running it in production. That is arguably the real finding.

Skills

The retooling problem nobody budgeted

Several comments make the same argument: the blocker is not the product, it is that Windows and VMware admins do not know KVM, and one org's SME advising against migration was the person whose skills were locked to VMware. Meanwhile the VMware certification job market is collapsing and VMUG lab licenses now sit behind an annual VCP-VCF exam.
